# 01 — Vision

## The one idea

> **Your child is not the problem.**

Everything on the page serves that sentence. UNHEARD sells a ₹1,200 box of
play-based activities to Indian parents of children aged 4–12 who learn
differently. The product is not mainly the cards and cord. It is a change in how
the parent sees their child, from "something is wrong" to "I can see how they
learn".

The page has to make a worried parent feel two things within one scroll:

1. **Safe.** This is calm and credible. It isn't a gimmick, and it isn't a
   clinic.
2. **Hopeful.** This is about my child's strengths. It is not a list of deficits.

## Who it is for

**The parent** reads the page. They are usually a mother, often anxious, and may
already have heard "lazy", "careless" or "can't sit still" from a school. They
need clarity, honesty about what the kit is and isn't, a visible price, and a
real person behind it.

**The child** is never the reader but is always present. The page should feel
like something a child would want to open: a sticker book, not a medical
pamphlet. That warmth is what tells the parent "this is play, not testing".

So the brief is a **sticker book a parent can trust**. It should look playful
and read with integrity.

## Why it looks like this

The original site was bland because it borrowed a generic "premium notebook"
look that belonged to nobody. The founder already had a visual identity: her
pitch deck. It has chunky slate capitals with a blush shadow, rough dry-brush
paint splashes, a red scribble, yellow sparkles and flat illustrated Indian
kids. It is warm, handmade and clearly hers.

The vision was to **take the deck seriously as a brand** and translate it to the
web properly. Copying the slides was not the goal. Concretely:

- The deck's **look** (caps, splashes, sparkles, kids) becomes the page's
  personality layer.
- **Digital Lavender** (`#42326E` → `#E0D4FC`) becomes the brand and action
  colour. It is calm, low-anxiety and trustworthy, and it adds the seriousness a
  parent needs before spending money.
- Modern family-product patterns from the references give the playfulness a
  professional structure. These are Greenlight's rounded bento cards, Klarna's
  bold headline scale and colour blocks, and Loop/Haoqi's monospace micro-labels.

**Deck warmth on top, product-grade structure underneath.**

## The story the page tells

The sections follow the founder's own pitch order. The page reads like her
argument, not a template.

| # | Section | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero: "Your child is not the problem." | Land the idea. Show the real kit and kids' voices. |
| 2 | Ticker: Observe · Understand · Support · Grow | Rhythm break that states the method. |
| 3 | Problem: "Judged before they're understood." | Name the pain. "Lazy" gets crossed out to reveal what was really there. |
| 4 | How it works: four stages | Make the method concrete and doable at home. |
| 5 | Learners: "Different isn't difficult." | Reframe difference as a way in, never a category. |
| 6 | Kit: "One box. Five tools." | Show the product and the price. This is the grape night section, the visual peak. |
| 7 | Three ways in | Workshops, kit and school pilot, honestly labelled by status. |
| 8 | Complementary, not clinical | Position against therapy, plus the "not a diagnosis" disclaimer. |
| 9 | Story | Prisha, in her own words: "the tool I wish my teachers had." |
| 10 | Final CTA: "Start with one afternoon." | Lower the commitment to a single afternoon. |

## Principles

1. **Strengths before labels.** Every label the page shows is crossed out and
   replaced with what was really going on.
2. **Honest by default.** Nothing implied that isn't true: no fake checkout,
   no invented stats, no "available" on things that are still a waitlist.
   Quotes are the founder's real words.
3. **Play in the decoration, clarity in the content.** Splashes, stickers and
   tilts live around the text, never on top of it. Body text stays calm and
   high-contrast.
4. **One person made this.** Tape, tilted photo cards, a hand-drawn underline.
   The page should feel made by a student who cares, at product quality.
5. **Never the puzzle piece.** The child is not incomplete.
